## 1. Seafire IIC / L IIC / III performance points

Sources: Armouredcarriers.com and related public summaries of A&AEE / service trials.

**Seafire F.Mk IIC (Merlin 45/46):**  
- Max speed: 363 mph at 7300 ft; 398 mph at 21 000 ft  
- Rate of climb: 4900 ft/min at 4800 ft  
- Notes: 6% heavier than IB; ~15 mph slower than predecessor due to C wing and strengthening.

**Seafire L.Mk IIC (Merlin 32):**  
- Max speed: 316 mph SL; 335 mph at 6000 ft  
- Rate of climb: 4600 ft/min up to 6000 ft (emergency boost)  
- Notes: Low-altitude optimized; superior initial climb and acceleration vs Hellcat/Corsair below ~10 000 ft. Eric Brown commentary: initial climb rate and acceleration "little short of magnificent."

**Seafire Mk III (Merlin 55/55M):**  
- Max speed: ~352 mph (various sources)  
- Rate of climb: ~3460–4800 ft/min depending on mark and altitude  
- Notes: First folding-wing production version. Performance generally similar to corresponding Spitfire but with naval equipment drag.

## 2. Spitfire XIV / Griffon quantitative points

Sources: Historyofwar.org, AFDU summaries, and related public references.

**Spitfire Mk XIV (Griffon 65/66):**  
- Max speed: 446 mph at 25 400 ft (prototype / published figures); ~439–446 mph range in service summaries  
- Rate of climb: ~4580 ft/min (summary); superior to Mk IX at all heights (30–35 mph faster in level flight)  
- Operational ceiling: ~40 000 ft  
- Notes: Best rate of climb yet seen in early 1944 tests. AFDU comparisons showed clear superiority over Mk IX in speed and climb. Manoeuvrability not significantly improved over earlier marks (airframe-limited).

## 3. RAE Farnborough ARC R&M 2535 (high-speed tunnel)

Source: Public extracts of R&M 2535 (April 1945) — High Speed Wind Tunnel Tests on Models of Four Single-Engined Fighters (Spitfire, Spiteful, Attacker and Mustang).

Key quantitative notes:  
- Spitfire model: large drag rise above Mach ~0.7; no important trim/stability changes up to Mach 0.775 in related flight tests.  
- Spiteful: higher critical Mach number than Spitfire for drag rise; small performance gain expected from compressibility suppression alone because Spitfire drag rise is initially gradual.  
- Mustang: included for comparison; elevator trim and drag curves compared to Spitfire.  
- General: lift, drag, and pitching moment measured; nose-down trim change at higher Mach numbers common across models.  

Full page-by-page public HTML transcription is limited; the above are the main performance-relevant points available without the original PDF.
