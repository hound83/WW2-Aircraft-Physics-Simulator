04 / Cross-aircraft check — one solver, two source-reported Mustang weights

This is the deciding test, not a new fitted aircraft model. At 15,000 ft, the P-47B's open-flap climb test supplies the one fitting observation for a configuration-loss term. Four other P-47B altitudes are withheld. The P-51B receives no imported P-47 loss term; its own reported automatic cooling shutters are retained. Its level-speed-calibrated polar is frozen.

Both concern P-51B-15 43-24777 and are not independent aircraft/test programmes. The model uses each source's stated takeoff weight as its scenario mass, without claiming that this equals exact airborne mass.

The predicted optimum TAS in the table is therefore a model prediction, never a measured speed. Result: Candidate B better matches the Mustang's absolute climb; candidate C is better on the P-47's withheld climb tests and the Mustang's rating-induced improvement. The evidence therefore does not yet justify a single winner. No numerical coefficient was changed to make either aircraft fit.

05 / P-51B: predict the climb improvement without tuning

Wright Field Eng-47-1749-A gives 3,820 ft/min at 67 inHg and 4,380 ft/min at 75 inHg, both at 2,200 ft. We hold the aircraft's existing speed-calibrated drag polar and all propeller assumptions unchanged. The common solver computes best climb at each rating using the source-reported 9,335 lb takeoff weight as a scenario; speed-calibrated drag remains frozen. The instantaneous airborne mass is unknown.

The historical report does not give climbing airspeed in its text. Model speeds are optimization outputs, not measured flight speeds. Consequently the absolute-rate check is provisional. No P-47B fitted cooling-loss coefficient was transferred to the Mustang. No model uses these climb observations to recalibrate drag or propeller efficiency.

The report also states a +580 ft/min increase at 15,700 ft in high blower. Our 75-inHg high-blower power dataset does not cover that altitude. That second comparison remains unavailable until the relevant engine chart is transcribed; we do not extrapolate.

06 / P-47B: stop using high-speed horsepower for climbing

Wright Field reported different engine manifold pressures and cooling settings in level-speed and climb tests of the same P-47B. We now correct shaft power using the observed climb manifold pressure (approximate MAP-to-power scaling), then fit one aggregate climb-configuration loss at 15,000 ft. Every other altitude is held out from that correction.

The equivalent added drag coefficient is not a measured cooling-drag coefficient. It can include open-flap cooling drag, low-speed propeller inefficiency, uncorrected power or test-method differences. Its validity across different aircraft or other engine settings is unproven. No drag coefficient was refitted to the four withheld climb points.

10 / What is known versus assumed?

Scientific limitations and next validation work

Critical: The model fits CD₀ using one reported maximum-speed anchor for each aircraft. This exactly constrains one equation but cannot independently determine both propulsive efficiency and parasite drag. The assumed Oswald parameter determines induced drag and sustained turns. The maximum-lift coefficients are provisional and therefore the stall boundary and turn curves are illustrative.

Bf 109: secondary approximate engine horsepower at 0–15,748 ft only; its sea-level 325 mph is not a matched primary test specimen, and Greg's screenshot does not provide a validated individual power-versus-altitude curve. Above this range no calculated comparison is offered.

P-47: Greg's high-power P-47 spreadsheet and Greg's high-altitude P-47B graph are separate datasets. Do not transfer 2,210 propulsive hp to the P-47B. The P-47B sourced speed tests are with cooling flaps flush, while climb testing opens shutters; no configuration-specific cooling drag yet.

Stall / airspeed: P-47 manual 115 mph IAS and P-51B flight-test 86 mph IAS are not calibrated EAS. We do not reverse-engineer exact CLmax from uncorrected IAS. CAS and TAS conversions are subsonic ISA theoretical; true IAS needs equipment/position calibration.

Propeller: Candidate A/B/C models are exploratory. Candidate C has not reproduced the six-propeller controlled P-47B experiment; no claim of NACA-validated blade efficiency is made.

Next: Validate independent climb and power-required at exact test speeds and cooling positions; seek historical low-speed CAS calibration and CLmax; test Greg's table sensitivity versus geometric e; constrain propeller class maps with P-47 propeller-swap tests; add Bf 109 source-specific test curves for higher altitudes; preserve uncertainty ranges before making ranked aircraft claims.
