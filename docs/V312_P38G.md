# v3.12 research build | P-38G-1 and symmetric multi-engine propulsion

The comparison panel adds P-38G-1 42-12687 and compares it with the existing P-47B 41-5902 model at six altitudes from 5,000 to 30,000 ft. Both are clean and all engines operate. The model applies faired/reference cooling for level speed, open cooling for climb and 175 mph EAS for both aircraft in climb. Each aircraft retains its own weight and rating schedule. For the P-38G comparison the level-speed mass is fixed at 13,550 lb and the climb mass at 13,900 lb. The P-47B uses 12,565 lb and military power.

## Data and methods

- [Wright Field FS-M-19-1538-A](../references/p38g-42-12687.json) contains the original scan link and hash, six transcribed level speeds and eight transcribed climb rates. The report's BHP is derived from engine curves per engine. Level weights are historical estimates. The report supplies only takeoff weight for climb. Ceiling rows lack power and remain unscored.
- Per-engine shaft power and RPM are independent of whole-aircraft drag. One 11.5 ft disk is evaluated at each engine's power and 1,500 propeller rpm (3,000 engine rpm, 2:1 reduction). Symmetry makes both results identical. Sum propulsive power from both disks, then subtract required power once. The momentum model assumes common inflow with no propeller interaction and uses generic loss parameters. Lift and span efficiency remain assumptions.
- Fit CD0 to 346.5 mph TAS at 5,000 ft and 44.7 inHg. With that polar fixed, fit one nonnegative effective drag area for wide-open cooling at 10,000 ft. The twin fit yields CD0 0.02589 and an added 0.04605 m2. The climb replay uses each reported TAS and takeoff weight. The solver uses ISA. Power is linearly interpolated between source nodes within each schedule.
- For the control, put the same total BHP into one disk of the same diameter and RPM. Refit the same speed and climb anchors under the same bounds. Its open-cooling fit requests -0.27959 m2, so the nonnegative constraint sets the added area to zero. It is a numerical comparison of disk loading.

## Reserved checks

| Observation | Count | Twin mean absolute error | One-disk control |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ordinary level speed, 14,700-30,000 ft | 4 | 6.09 mph TAS | 5.93 mph TAS |
| Open-cooling climb, sea level-35,000 ft | 7 | 173 ft/min | 141 ft/min |

The twin configuration slightly increases both reserved-point errors while representing the correct propeller loading and allowing positive open-cooling drag. The reserved rows share the fitting report and supply the BHP used in each replay. They are checks of the coupled model under stated conditions, with no independent accuracy estimate. The separate 5,000 ft / 48 inHg observation is 355.5 mph TAS versus 356.1 predicted and is outside the ordinary score. The report gives a temperature restriction at 14,700 ft for that power.

At 22,600 ft Wright Field reports 404 mph TAS at 1,225 bhp per engine (2,450 total). The twin model predicts 409.3 mph. Across the ordinary speed checks, residuals are positive, from +1.9 to +9.7 mph. Climb residuals range from -110 ft/min at 35,000 ft to +411 ft/min at sea level. At 20,000 ft the report gives 3,280 ft/min at 219 mph TAS and the model gives 3,384 ft/min.

Refitting the cooling term at 6,000 or 15,000 ft instead of 10,000 ft yields 0.046-0.113 m2 across the three fits. On the five climb checks shared by all splits, the MAE ranges from 193 to 228 ft/min. The predicted P-38G climb at 20,000 ft and 175 mph EAS ranges from 3,157 to 3,243 ft/min. This is sensitivity to the fit point, with the level polar and power schedule held fixed.

## Model comparison at 20,000 ft

| Aircraft | Level speed (mph TAS) | Climb at 175 mph EAS (ft/min) | Climb shaft hp total / per engine |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P-38G-1 | 399.5 | 3,243 | 2,390 / 1,195 |
| P-47B | 403.7 | 2,162 | 2,000 / 2,000 |

The estimated speed difference is smaller than the P-38G's reserved level-speed MAE. The climb comparison uses a shared EAS, distinct from the TAS schedules in the two historical trials. Independent, matched evidence and bounds for installed propeller efficiency, shaft power, cooling drag and climb weight are needed for a 5:1 claim. The Wright Field report provides no matched P-38G stall or turn score.

Reproduce with `npm run diagnose:fighter-comparison`. See `p38g` in the generated `validation/current/fighter-comparison.json` for replays, fit roles and comparisons. `npm run check` also verifies the frozen import and the original four-aircraft benchmark.
