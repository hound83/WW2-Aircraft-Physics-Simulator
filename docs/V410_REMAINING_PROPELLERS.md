# Remaining fighter propellers

The second propeller pass covers the five fighters outside the [Corsair and Thunderbolt pass](V410_PROPELLER_REFERENCE.md). The v4.10.0-rc.2 research candidate uses a separate level-flight shaft-power schedule and a fitted tip-Mach loss for the P-38G-1 at dry Military power. The other four candidates keep their current propulsion models on the evidence below.

## Five-aircraft results

## P-38G-1 Military level-flight replay

Wright Field FS-M-19-1538-A B.1 lists 3,000 engine rpm with faired oil and coolant shutters. It reports shaft BHP from engine charts and estimated level-flight weights. B.4 uses open cooling and a different altitude-power schedule. At 22,600 ft the level table gives 1,225 BHP per engine. The previous live calculation used 1,140.4 BHP per engine there from the climb schedule. The new calculation uses each engine's share before summing useful power and subtracting airframe drag once. Above the 44.7-inHg critical height it uses the reported pressure and turbo or temperature limits through 30,000 ft. [Wright Field B.1 and B.4](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/p-38/p-38.html) · [Source transcription and scan hash](../references/p38g-42-12687.json)

The existing 5,000-ft drag anchor gives a helical tip Mach of 0.94465. The model multiplies its current useful-power estimate by \(\exp[-3.325\,(\max(0,M_\mathrm{tip}-0.94465))^2]\) at the center of the reference domain. The coefficient fits the 22,600-ft run at its reported power and mass. Drag stays at the existing calibration. The fit is an equivalent high-speed loss, with attribution to the propeller conditional on the drag and shaft-power inputs. NACA L7D10 Table III runs 24-10 and 24-9 provide a mechanism check. The tested three-blade Curtiss on a P-47D-28 dropped from 81.2% to 76.2% between tip Mach 1.001 and 1.044 at similar power coefficients of 0.183 and 0.180. The advance ratios and blade drawings differ from the P-38G. These measurements constrain plausibility and supply no P-38G map. [NACA L7D10](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20050019331)

The core applies at 5,000–30,000 ft and advance ratio 1.75–2.10 in dry Military at 3,000 rpm with clean cooling. Power and efficiency fade together over up to 1,000 ft or 0.25 advance ratio. In the altitude fade the power difference comes from the nearest report boundary. The additional tip loss is capped at tip Mach 1.09. Those transition widths and cap are engineering choices. The level reference is selected in the shared benchmark, including the applicable parts of clean acceleration and turn curves.

Refitting at 24,400 ft gives a coefficient of 4.047. On the two common reserved points at 14,700 and 30,000 ft the 22,600 and 24,400 ft fits give 0.71 and 0.46 mph mean error respectively. The report conditions were available before this work. The reserved runs exclude the new fits and share their test campaign. No independent P-38G speed or direct propeller-efficiency test met the same conditions.

At part power, the 5,000-ft 290-BHP and 24,400-ft 410-BHP cases imply 137% and 125% useful-to-shaft power at the existing polar. The two empirical speed interpolations remain useful at their recorded altitudes. The figures point to a coupled part-power drag, cooling or chart-power mismatch. Preserve those 13 observations and the implied efficiencies for later diagnosis. The seven open-cooling climb checks still average 173 ft/min absolute error, with the 10,000-ft cooling anchor excluded.

## Operating comparisons

The [frozen v4.10.0-rc.1 scorecard](../references/analysis/v410-rc1-operating-baseline.json) has 84 steady states, 42 timed accelerations and 14 timed climbs. The six other aircraft reproduce their prior values. Across the grid the P-38G's stall, maximum climb, timed climb, peak turn rates and result availability match the baseline. Its modeled Military 180–300 mph EAS acceleration at 10,000 ft moves from 95.17 to 93.12 seconds. At 30,000 ft the incomplete run reaches 241.08 mph EAS, down from 246.68. These are modeled elapsed times and reached speeds.

The dry Combat P-38G and Spitfire at 30,000 ft and 50% fuel are now a practical level-speed tie at 394.61 and 394.44 mph TAS. The old modeled Lightning lead was 9.64 mph. At fixed drag and tip loss, a ±2% scenario on the Lightning's chart BHP gives 391.95–397.21 mph, still within the 6 km/h comparison margin. This is an engineering sensitivity. The independent historical evidence for timed acceleration, powered turn and 5:1 discrimination remains open. [Practical comparison margins](V49_TIMED_MATCHUPS.md#practical-ties-and-chart-evidence)

## Source checks for follow-up

- **P-38G.** NACA WR L-483 Table I lists Curtiss 88996 and 89303 blade families under activity factors. The table gives blade geometry information. Its generic efficiency charts do not provide an installed curve for 42-12687. T.O. 03-20-1 also indexes the contemporary propeller installations. The P-38F 41-2293 acceptance-report transcription lists the same 88996-18/89303-18 pair with V-1710-49/-53, faired level cooling and gun cover plates. The different engine and fit, and lack of a checked original scan, leave it a transfer lead. [NACA WR L-483](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930093644) · [T.O. 03-20-1](https://pubs.st.aero/library/books/ocr/91107070-cb64-4353-8504-7792de2111c3.pdf) · [41-2293 report transcription](https://www.alternatehistory.com/forum/threads/wi-naca-modified-p-38.419398/page-10)
- **P-51B.** A&AEE Mustang IV TK589 corroborates K.6523A-24 and the V-1650-7 in low blower. Table I reports 396 mph TAS at 10,300 ft and +18 lb, corrected to 9,000 lb. It used six guns, faired racks and an 8½-inch radiator gap. The two test campaigns give an installation comparison and carry different drag and boost states. TK589 does not extend to high blower. [Wright Field 43-24777](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/p-51/p51b-24777.html) · [A&AEE TK589 §§2–4 and Table I](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/p-51/tk589.html)
- **Spitfire LF IX.** A&AEE MA.648 used the same Merlin 66 but the R12/4F5/4 wooden-blade Rotol and a new intake. Its comparison table records high-blower full-throttle heights of 19,300–22,100 ft across four installations. The RA.10061 metal blade remains the target. DSIR 23/15215 indexes a Rotol metal Spitfire tunnel report. Retrieve its test drawings before transferring coefficients. [BS.543 Tables II and V](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitLF-HF-mkIX.html) · [MA.648 §4.2](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitLFMkIX_MA648.html) · [DSIR catalog](https://enginehistory.org/References/UKNA/Prop-DSIR.shtml)
- **Fw 190 A-4.** WNr 783 Nr. 5 supplies a 2,700-rpm, 1.42-ata low-blower curve with the internal intake. Its 607 km/h sea-level point used 1.70 ata in the full-blower-pressure experiment. Keep those cases apart when checking the 2,700-rpm model. Nr. 1 also reports 670 km/h at 6,800 m for WNr 528 with the long intake. Use it as a separate-airframe lead pending matched engine and test configuration. [WNr 783 Nr. 1](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/fw190/a4/fw190-783-nr1.html) · [Nr. 2](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/fw190/fw190-783-nr2.html) · [Nr. 5](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/fw190/a4/fw190-783-nr5.html)
- **Bf 109 F-4.** Messerschmitt 109 19 L 42 Blatt 2 identifies the three-blade VDM 9-12010 A, 3 m, paired with DB 601 E in G-1 WNr 14026. The installation manual confirms 1.685:1 gearing on the DB 601 E F-4. Rechlin 15023/41 supplies times over large climb bands with incomplete power conditions. Retrieve the DB 601 E-G engine manual power chart and a rated F-4 run before changing the propeller model. [Messerschmitt original](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/VB-109-19-L-42.pdf) · [German source register](research/WW2_GERMAN_FIGHTER_ARCHIVE_2026-11-11.md#bf-109-f-4) · [Engine manual inventory](https://www.maparchive.ru/nara-doc/Field_and_Technica_Manuals/Field_and_Technical_Manuals.pdf)
