# Four-fighter comparison

Open **Compare four fighters** in the standalone research build. The same report is embedded in the browser, downloadable as JSON, and generated by `npm run diagnose:fighter-comparison` at `validation/current/fighter-comparison.json`. Historical speed and climb residuals are shown by altitude band with the scored and observed counts. See the [historical scorecard](FLIGHT_SCORECARD.md) and [separate archival checks](INDEPENDENT_FLIGHT_CHECKS.md) for the detailed evidence and differences in conditions.

## Fixed model states

ISA, 175 mph EAS, reference configuration, profile P and nominal default rating for each aircraft. Climb is calculated at 175 mph EAS. Top speed is a separate level-flight equilibrium. Engine calculations are unavailable for the default F-4 at 27,000 ft. Its provisional one-g stall calculation remains available because it uses weight and lift rather than engine power. IAS requires a matched calibration.

## Engineering reference comparisons

- **Mustang workbook.** At 20,800 ft, 250 mph TAS, 9,240 lb and 67 inHg in high blower, both calculations use 1,390 shaft hp. The workbook uses a fixed 0.80 propeller efficiency and profile P calculates 0.8825. Propulsive power is 1,112.0 vs 1,226.7 hp and required power is 405.6 vs 410.4 hp. Sustained load is 2.13 vs 2.39 g. These are two engineering calculations. The frozen workbook reference is sheet Greg Speed Table, row 31, cells AC–AF and AN.
- **Greg P-47B.** The seven approximate screenshot readings at 27,000 ft have an unspecified mph basis. With CAS assumed, mean absolute differences are 0.153 g for the working sustained limit, 0.191 g with the inferred 120 mph sea-level stall, and 0.134 g for the power limit alone. The corresponding EAS interpretations give 0.111, 0.142 and 0.093 g. A smaller residual under one axis interpretation does not establish its identity. The alternate stall only affects the first two CAS points. It does not supply an IAS correction for the historical 41-5902 stall measurement.
