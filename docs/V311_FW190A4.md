# v3.11 research build: Fw 190 A-4 and installed power on altitude

The fifth aircraft is **Fw 190 A-4 W.Nr. 783** with the long external intake, BMW 801 D-2/20208, 3,870 kg takeoff weight and combat power at 2,400 rpm. Open **Fw 190 A-4 vs Spitfire** in the standalone browser build. Choose an altitude from sea level to 10,000 m and a speed from 150, 175 or 225 mph EAS. Level speed is solved separately. The fixed-speed climb and sustained load are engineering estimates in reference configuration. In the historical climb check, the cooling flaps are open and the model chooses the climb speed.

## Results

| Same-report reserved check | Static power, same installation correction | Installed power | Change in MAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Level speed, 7 points | 42.0 km/h | 12.5 km/h | −70% |
| Open-flap climb, 8 points | 303 ft/min | 164 ft/min | −46% |

The speed error remains −32 km/h at 10,000 m. The open-flap climb error is +465 ft/min at sea level and −75 ft/min at 10,000 m. The closed-cooling installation correction and speed-dependent power are both material. Using speed-dependent power alone, without the fast-flight correction, gives 41.3 km/h speed MAE. The comparison table uses the same drag correction on both sides to isolate the change in power model. These are reserved points from the **same factory report**. They do not establish independent historical validation.

At 6,000 m the browser comparison gives 633 km/h for the Fw 190 and 641 km/h for the Spitfire. The Fw 190 chart reads approximately 631 km/h. At 175 mph EAS, their reference-configuration model climb rates are 2,483 and 4,374 ft/min respectively. These fixed-speed rates are a separate comparison from the open-flap best-climb checks. The two aircraft use their own reference weights and ratings.

## Evidence and model

- **Flight tests.** The compact [digitized source ledger](../references/fw190a4-783.json) retains both original graph links and SHA-256 hashes. We manually read the long-intake CINA speed and open-cooling climb curves against their axes, preserving approximate graph-reading tolerances of ±4 km/h, ±0.35 m/s and ±0.025 ata. These tolerances cover plot reading, not historical test uncertainty. Treat the short-intake and W.Nr. 665 data as separate configurations. [Factory report 783/1](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/fw190/a4/fw190-783-nr1.html)
- **Power.** The BMW 801 D manual gives 1,520 PS at 700 m on the low blower and 1,320 PS at 5,300 m on the high blower at the combat rating without ram. Convert PS to mechanical hp. Hold capped shaft power within each blower branch and scale below the cap by inlet pressure relative to the flight critical point, with 0.85 ram recovery and unit pressure exponent. The low blower uses the graph breakpoint near 2,000 m and 575 km/h. The high blower uses the reported 6,950 m and 652 km/h point. The graph indicates a change at about 3,000 m. The static control uses the no-ram critical heights and static pressure. The model rejects unsupported ratings and altitudes outside the declared domain. The engine-type manual transfer and inferred pressure response are engineering inputs; installed shaft torque was not recorded. [BMW 801 D power table, p. 11](https://spruemaster.com/wp-content/gallery/manual/Focke-Wulf-Fw190_A5-A6_Pilot-manual.pdf)
- **Drag, propeller and cooling.** Use the production geometry and 3.3 m VDM-family propeller, nominal 0.54 gearing, shared propeller solver and provisional e = 0.78 and CLmax = 1.4. Sea-level speed anchors the polar. The two inlet points also anchor the fast-flight installation correction using the existing Mach-gated formulation. The low-blower correction ramps from zero to −0.00271 Cd0, the high blower uses −0.00442. These are aggregate residuals that may include propeller, cooling or shaft-power error. Use the 5,000 m open/closed climb difference to infer 0.171 m² of added drag area at the model climb speed. Historical climb speed is unavailable in this graph. The remaining points are excluded from these anchor calculations.
- **Sensitivity.** Retain the transparent linear pressure scaling as the main model. The report export includes exploratory shaft-power exponents of 0.85 and 2/3. Those alternatives change the 10,000 m speed to roughly 610 and 637 km/h and the climb to 553 and 826 ft/min, against chart values of 618 km/h and 423 ft/min. The alternatives illustrate the dependence on the pressure-to-shaft-power assumption. They do not define an error bound. Independent propeller efficiency, corrected stall and historical turn evidence remain open.
