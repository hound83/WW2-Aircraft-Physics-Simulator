**Start with the G-6/Trop climb and cooling sheets and reconcile the Bf 109 E / Spitfire Va records already collected.** Both jobs can change what the next aircraft comparison can use immediately. ## What the simulator can check today

The separate November 16 early-war extract contains **522 rows in 78 series**, with 40 source records, 30 of them referenced by a series. Some rows describe test conditions or repeat an earlier observation. ## Three changes to the data

1. **Link repeated readings to one test.** The early-war extract is outside the 25-pack catalog build. Comparing all tabulated fields after normalizing column names, all nine X.4922 level-speed rows and all 19 non-origin climb rows already match catalog entries. The extra climb entry is the zero-time origin. Map these 28 readings to the existing observation IDs. Keep the newly recovered cooling, loading, companion-aircraft and conflicting-source evidence where it adds information. Reconcile all 78 series as new evidence, existing evidence, alternate transcription, derived result or source lead. [Earlier intake](../../references/research/ww2_priority_aircraft_2026-11-13.json) · [November 16 handoff](WW2_EARLY_WAR_FLIGHT_TESTS_2026-11-14.md)
2. **Track what each comparison can actually use.** Keep the existing primary/secondary/calculated dossier percentages as an inventory. Add a per-aircraft, per-metric view with the best applicable trial, the speed/altitude range, the number of independent trials, and the missing condition that would change the verdict. Distinguish fitted, reserved within the same trial, separate trial, transferred and model-only evidence. The catalog already has 67 document-lineage groups. Extend them to trial and run where identifiable. Two scans of one sheet and two time intervals from one climb each share a parent. Reserve an entire different run or specimen before fitting whenever possible. [Evidence intake contract](MINIMUM_VIABLE_SIM_DATA.md) · [Current coverage weights](WW2_FIGHTER_SIM_INPUTS_V1.md)
3. **Keep the reported value alongside every conversion or transfer.** In the catalog's 288 stall-speed rows, 171 are labeled reported IAS, 85 have an unresolved speed basis, 16 are reported CAS and 16 carry provisional corrections. For a stall, record speed channel, calibration range, mass at the event, configuration and power. For a plotted trace, add the figure, axis calibration, trace identity and read-off uncertainty. Show only the segments that depend on a donor or extrapolation as provisional. Keep unknown fields explicit. A handbook value or a secondary transcription can support an indicated-speed comparison while a corrected lift estimate awaits evidence. [Stall donors and worked transfers](../../references/research/STALL_DONOR_SHORTLIST_2026-11-13.md)

## Research order

| Priority | Research and source leads | Deliverable and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. G-6/Trop and the three Mk IX profiles** | For WNr 16476, Messerschmitt report 109 10 L 43 Blatt 6 plots climb time, climb rate and the indicated-speed schedule. Blatt 7 plots radiator opening and coolant temperature by altitude. The test used 3,260 kg, 1.3 ata, 2,600 rpm and the sand filter. The plotted indicated-speed schedule drops above 6 km. For BF.274, keep the Merlin 61 and the Normal/Combat gear schedules. Use the paired BS.543 Merlin 66 and BS.551 Merlin 70 report to investigate the **30,000-ft HF advantage of 18 mph observed versus 29.7 mph modeled**. Prioritize installed power, propeller and gear conditions around that discrepancy. [G-6 original, PDF pages 7–8](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/me109/g/g_files/VB-109-10-L-43.pdf#page=7) · [BF.274](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitFmkIX_BF274.html) · [LF/HF paired trial](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitLF-HF-mkIX.html) · [Comparison](../V413_AIRCRAFT_INTAKE.md) | Digitize the G-6 sheets once with their native axes. Distinguish plotted points from the fitted line. Report signed time and rate errors by altitude against the existing replay. One accepted time series would take climb-time comparison coverage from 11/12 to 12/12. Treat rate, time and cooling from that climb as related evidence. For the Mk IX pair, report how much of the 11.7 mph excess can be explained within the source bounds. |
| **2. Bf 109 E-3 WNr 1304 and Spitfire Va X.4922** | Stage the CEMA p. 4 speed series by radiator state and the eight French climb rows reproduced by the RAE. At 5,000 m, 520 and 570 km/h came with different manifold pressures, 870 and 880 mm. WNr 1792 supplies a separate low-altitude comparison. For X.4922, use the +9 lb/in², 6,450 lb test. Its measured level-speed series starts at 10,000 ft. Keep the low-altitude RAE Mk V curve as family evidence and W.3119's clean-glide lift as a conditional donor. [CEMA and RAE sheets](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/me109e.html) · [X.4922](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk5/spitmkVa_X4922.html) · [NACA W.3119 stall report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930092581) | Deliver two speed/climb dossiers with exact, donor and extrapolated segments marked, with the 28 known overlaps reconciled. Preserve the pressure difference in the cooling comparison. Look for actual stall-run mass and airspeed correction for the E, and exact low-altitude speed or stall for the Va. Keep applicability decisions separate for level speed, climb and turn. Follow with Mk I N.3171. |
| **3. Calibrated low-speed lift across families** | Start with P-51B-15 43-24777 and the full-span Spitfire IXs. The Mustang's Fig. 1 uses instrument-corrected IAS and its plotted scale starts near 100 mph, above the 86 mph reported stall. BF.274's position-error test covers 130–320 mph indicated. Search for a calibration or independent corrected reading that reaches the stall itself. Next try P-47D-30 43-3441. NACA L8A06 Figs. 49–50 give two glide-stall traces at 12,400 lb and 5,000 ft. [Mustang Fig. 1](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/p-51/p-51_files/P-51B_24777_Airspeed_Calibration.jpg) · [BF.274 test](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitFmkIX_BF274.html) · [P-47D-30 report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090022749) | Aim for one newly supported clean-stall check in each of two families. Identify the airspeed channel, mass, power, wing/tips and flap state. Use square-root weight scaling only between compatible aerodynamic configurations with a defensible corrected anchor. Give a range or retain the indicated-speed observation when an input is missing. |
| **4. Straight acceleration and drag during a turn** | The Ellis/Wheal Fig. 8 is readable and contains intermediate indicated-airspeed versus time curves for four restored aircraft. The current Mustang replay uses only two secondary-transcribed endpoints and finishes about 40 s early. Recover the curve for 45-11586 at 10,000 ft MSL and record the engine and load differences from wartime 44-15342. For MT-215, use the two published transcriptions to search the Finnish archives by serial, pilot and both reported dates. Seek a separate US or UK wartime trial with straight and turning speed histories under comparable conditions. [Original Fig. 8](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/com/others/endingtheargument_files/figure8.jpg) · [Authors' test conditions](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/com/others/endingtheargument.html) · [MT-215 transcriptions and date conflict](../../references/analysis/powered-turn-evidence-2026-11-13.json) · [Finnish Aviation Museum archives](https://ilmailumuseo.fi/en/kirjasto-arkisto/) | First show where the acceleration error grows along the restored Mustang's curve. As a visual check on the trace, it lies between 225 and 235 KIAS at 100 s. Record digitization uncertainty before using a narrower reading. For a historical turn claim, seek straight and loaded segments with speed, time, altitude change, load or heading, mass, rating and cooling, plus a reserved check. If only stall, glide or endpoint evidence exists, report the part of the maneuver it constrains. Give the 180° turn-plus-recovery result an independent check only when its complete measured sequence is available. |

**Use secondary sources actively.** Drix's E-3 table and Rocher's CEAM account give leads and alternative readings of the French work. The original CEMA table places its second 570 km/h point at 5,300 m, while Drix gives 5,500 m. Keep both readings with page and parent-trial links, and prefer the original for that ordinate. Apply the same rule to the two Kokko transcriptions and to a modern reproduction of an A&AEE table. Greg's polars for all 19 dossiers provide starting estimates. A second constraint at a substantially different lift coefficient is more informative for turn drag than another full-speed observation. ## When to move to the next batch

- **Finish the first handoff** with the G-6 digitization with a repeat read, the early-war series crosswalk, and the change in the number of profiles with a numerical climb comparison. Publish the distinct-trial counts separately. A verified duplicate, conflict or unsupported range is a useful outcome.
- **At the next handoff**, report signed residuals in mph, seconds or ft/min by aircraft and altitude band. State whether the ordering between aircraft survives plausible measurement, loading and transfer uncertainty. Preserve a same-report check under its own label. A 5:1 discrimination claim needs evidence at the claimed conditions.
- **For the following candidates**, use P-38G and F4U-4 as reference checks for the stall method and P-47B 41-5942 as a separate test of the 41-5902 work. F6F-3 04776 is the first expansion candidate if its stall calibration and run mass can be tied together. Roll-rate work can reuse the W.3119 force-limited measurements once pilot force, speed, altitude and wing configuration can be matched on a second aircraft.
