## Three changes to the data

1. **Link repeated readings to one test.** The early-war extract is outside the 25-pack catalog build. Comparing all tabulated fields after normalizing column names, all nine X.4922 level-speed rows and all 19 non-origin climb rows already match catalog entries. The extra climb entry is the zero-time origin. Map these 28 readings to the existing observation IDs. Keep the newly recovered cooling, loading, companion-aircraft and conflicting-source evidence where it adds information. Reconcile all 78 series as new evidence, existing evidence, alternate transcription, derived result or source lead. [Earlier intake](../../references/research/ww2_priority_aircraft_2026-11-13.json) · [November 16 handoff](WW2_EARLY_WAR_FLIGHT_TESTS_2026-11-14.md)
2. **Track what each comparison can actually use.** Keep the existing primary/secondary/calculated dossier percentages as an inventory. Add a per-aircraft, per-metric view with the best applicable trial, the speed/altitude range, the number of independent trials, and the missing condition that would change the verdict. Distinguish fitted, reserved within the same trial, separate trial, transferred and model-only evidence. The catalog already has 67 document-lineage groups. Extend them to trial and run where identifiable. Two scans of one sheet and two time intervals from one climb each share a parent. Reserve an entire different run or specimen before fitting whenever possible. [Evidence intake contract](MINIMUM_VIABLE_SIM_DATA.md) · [Current coverage weights](WW2_FIGHTER_SIM_INPUTS_V1.md)
3. **Keep the reported value alongside every conversion or transfer.** In the catalog's 288 stall-speed rows, 171 are labeled reported IAS, 85 have an unresolved speed basis, 16 are reported CAS and 16 carry provisional corrections. For a stall, record speed channel, calibration range, mass at the event, configuration and power. For a plotted trace, add the figure, axis calibration, trace identity and read-off uncertainty. Show only the segments that depend on a donor or extrapolation as provisional. Keep unknown fields explicit. A handbook value or a secondary transcription can support an indicated-speed comparison while a corrected lift estimate awaits evidence. [Stall donors and worked transfers](../../references/research/STALL_DONOR_SHORTLIST_2026-11-13.md)

## Combined milestone, altitude performance

Where does each aircraft have a measured speed or climb advantage, and how does that change with altitude, engine rating and cooling? Compare E-3 WNr 1304 with Va X.4922, then compare G-6/Trop WNr 16476 with F IX BF.274, LF IX BS.543 and HF IX BS.551. The LF/HF trial also tests a change of engine and propeller within the Spitfire family. Four aircraft already have operating profiles. The E-3 and Va have research dossiers ready for their first speed and climb comparisons.

## Combined milestone, turn entry through speed recovery

How much speed does the aircraft lose in the turn, and how long does it take to get it back? Combine the clean-stall and usable-lift records, drag under load and straight-line acceleration in one assessment. Start with the Mustang, full-span Spitfire IX and Bf 109 G-2 families. Use the P-47D-30 as a further low-speed check. Keep each donor, restored specimen and wartime airframe tied to its own trial.

| Evidence | Research to combine | Result to publish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Calibrated low-speed lift across families** | Start with P-51B-15 43-24777 and the full-span Spitfire IXs. The Mustang's Fig. 1 uses instrument-corrected IAS and its plotted scale starts near 100 mph, above the 86 mph reported stall. BF.274's position-error test covers 130–320 mph indicated. Search for a calibration or independent corrected reading that reaches the stall itself. Next try P-47D-30 43-3441. NACA L8A06 Figs. 49–50 give two glide-stall traces at 12,400 lb and 5,000 ft. [Mustang Fig. 1](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/p-51/p-51_files/P-51B_24777_Airspeed_Calibration.jpg) · [BF.274 test](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitFmkIX_BF274.html) · [P-47D-30 report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090022749) | Aim for one newly supported clean-stall check in each of two families. Identify the airspeed channel, mass, power, wing/tips and flap state. Use square-root weight scaling only between compatible aerodynamic configurations with a defensible corrected anchor. Give a range or retain the indicated-speed observation when an input is missing. |
| **Straight acceleration and drag during a turn** | Ellis and Wheal's printed Figure 8 gives one point per restored aircraft. Its axes are indicated speed at 60 s and time to maximum speed. Read the P-51D 45-11586 point at about 195–200 KIAS at 60 s, with graphical uncertainty. The existing wartime-profile replay reaches its transcribed target speed about 40 s early. Keep the installation differences and endpoint definitions alongside the comparison. For MT-215, use the two published transcriptions to search the Finnish archives by serial, pilot and both reported dates. Seek a separate US or UK wartime trial with straight and turning speed histories under comparable conditions. [Printed Figure 8, *Sport Aviation*, June 1990, p. 52](https://ww2aircraft.net/forum/attachments/eta-6-jpg.469533/) · [Authors' test conditions](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/com/others/endingtheargument.html) · [Existing Mustang replay](../V414_LEVEL_ACCELERATION_REPLAY.md) · [MT-215 transcriptions and date conflict](../../references/analysis/powered-turn-evidence-2026-11-13.json) · [Finnish Aviation Museum archives](https://ilmailumuseo.fi/en/kirjasto-arkisto/) | Check the Mustang at 60 s and at the finish under its stated conditions. The previously suggested 100-second reading came from a different online graphic. Further intermediate readings need a traceable source. For each wartime turn, recover speed, time, altitude change, load or heading, mass, rating and cooling. Reserve a separate run for evaluation where possible. |

**Finish with an evidence-backed comparison of turn exit speed and seconds to regain entry speed across two independent aircraft families wherever the trials allow it.** Show which records constrain lift, which constrain drag during the turn, and which constrain straight recovery. Keep a partial measurement usable on its own. Record the full 180°-plus-recovery historical check as open wherever the measured sequence is missing.

## Source interpretation and later candidates

**Use secondary sources actively.** Drix's E-3 table and Rocher's CEAM account give leads and alternative readings of the French work. The original CEMA table places its second 570 km/h point at 5,300 m, while Drix gives 5,500 m. Keep both readings with page and parent-trial links, and prefer the original for that ordinate. Apply the same rule to the two Kokko transcriptions and to a modern reproduction of an A&AEE table. Greg's polars for all 19 dossiers provide starting estimates. A second constraint at a substantially different lift coefficient is more informative for turn drag than another full-speed observation. [French source comparison](WW2_EARLY_WAR_FLIGHT_TESTS_2026-11-14.md) · [Greg drag data](WW2_GREG_DRAG_MODELS_2026-11-13.md) · [Mustang–Spitfire drag sensitivity](../V413_TURN_DRAG.md)

Report signed residuals by aircraft and altitude band. State whether the ordering between aircraft survives plausible measurement, loading and transfer uncertainty. Preserve a same-report check under its own label. A 5:1 discrimination claim needs evidence at the claimed conditions.

Use P-38G and F4U-4 as reference checks for the stall method and P-47B 41-5942 as a separate test of the 41-5902 work. F6F-3 04776 is the first expansion candidate if its stall calibration and run mass can be tied together. Roll-rate work can reuse the W.3119 force-limited measurements once pilot force, speed, altitude and wing configuration can be matched on a second aircraft.
