**25 November 2026 · Proposed milestone · Simulator 4.15.0-rc.1 · Fighter Sim Inputs 1.11.0**

## 14-aircraft performance benchmark

**Goal.** From the Normal / Combat benchmark, select any two of 14 aircraft, see each aircraft’s supported performance curves, and open the flight tests behind the comparison. The same build adds the Bf 109 E-3 and Spitfire Va, a factory-climb replay for the G-6/Trop, and recorded stages of turn and recovery for two aircraft families.

*W Simulator work v6* is the twelve-profile baseline. The live reference build is `4f723c0f`. Main at `11395120` already contains the six-aircraft altitude comparison and the G-6 pressure investigation. Their measurements and source identifiers feed the benchmark. ### Work order and research decisions

Start with per-aircraft curve availability and connect the existing comparison rows to the selectors. In parallel, investigate the G-6 climb, gather the maneuver evidence and qualify the E-3 and Va inputs. As each replay and profile becomes ready, integrate it into the same benchmark.

- **G-6 climb.** Seek installed DB 605 A power or torque at 2,600 rpm from 8 to 10 km, or an independently flown filtered G-6/Trop with comparable records. At 4–6 km, seek a radiator-drag or propeller-efficiency measurement at climb speed. Compare both altitude bands and the measured level-speed points before adopting an operating input. Where independent evidence remains insufficient, carry the pressure alternatives into the release as labeled diagnostic replays.
- **Turn and recovery.** Seek the original MT-215 sheet and an independent British or US wartime family with straight and loaded speed histories, rating, altitude and run weight. Verify low-speed airspeed calibration where usable lift sets the limit. Label a recovery time reconstructed from separate runs as an estimate. A historical claim for the complete maneuver requires a continuous recorded turn and recovery. Test proposed changes to shared drag or lift on a reserved run or condition and across the fleet.
- **Early-war aircraft.** Check the E-3’s run weight, climb schedule and airspeed correction, and the Va’s installed Merlin 45 power, propeller behavior and low-altitude speed evidence. Use transferred stall data only with compatible wing geometry, configuration and corrected airspeed. Both aircraft count toward the 14-profile completion target.

### Definition of done

The complete milestone is one research-candidate build with all four capabilities accessible from the benchmark.

1. With the original twelve profiles, check all **66 pairs in both selector orders** at 10,000 ft, dry Combat power, 50% internal fuel, clean turn/level cooling and high-altitude estimates off, with the aircraft filter set to All. Expect **21 pairs with two** sustained-turn curves, **35 with one**, and **10 with a reason for each missing curve**. All 66 retain two climb and two acceleration curves.
2. With both new operating profiles included, check **91 distinct pairs in both orders (182 selections)**. Verify the supported ranges for the 25 additional pairs. Change units, pass an altitude limit and return. The plots and selected-speed readouts must agree throughout.
3. In the same build, open the six-aircraft comparison, reproduce the Spitfire and G-6 checks above, and follow the measured maneuver stages for two families. Confirm the measurements, assumptions, residuals and missing stages are visible in the live and downloadable simulator.
4. Compare the original twelve-profile scorecard before and after, identify any numerical change in the four reference aircraft, and run the numerical regression and interactive browser checks. Verify the published research candidate against the tested build. [Repository workflow](../WORKFLOW.md)

The Mk I N.3171 dossier and the W.3119 force-limited roll measurements remain follow-on research leads. ### 3. Second-sweep research in priority order

#### A. Bf 109 G-6/Trop WNr 16476 — explain the change in climb error with altitude

Messerschmitt report 109 10 L 43 plots elapsed time, climb rate and forward speed on **Blatt 7**, with radiator opening and coolant temperature on **Blatt 8** (PDF pages 7 and 8). The report text gives a nominal 260 km/h indicated, 2,600 rpm, 1.30 ata and a 3,260 kg takeoff mass. The plotted true-speed schedule rises from approximately 260 km/h at sea level to 447 km/h at 10 km. The earlier roadmap misidentified the sheet numbers and described a falling indicated-speed schedule. Use the true-speed curve for the replay and retain the reported nominal indicated speed as a separate field. [Original climb sheet](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/me109/g/g_files/me-109g6-16476-pg7-1400.jpg) · [Cooling sheet](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/me109/g/g_files/me-109g6-16476-pg8.jpg) · [Report text, section 2](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/me109/g/me109g-16476.html)

The merged data milestone digitized these sheets. I ran its first checkpoint, `09b39362`, against a clean build of the published simulator. Eleven climb-rate points and ten elapsed-time points share one campaign. The current model climbs about **388 ft/min too fast at 4 km** and **303 ft/min too slowly at 10 km**. The elapsed-time residual averages **−2.0 s**, hiding a change of sign from **−39.7 s at 8 km** to **+127.6 s at 10 km**. The estimated chart-reading allowances for those two elapsed points are ±12 s and ±24 s. The report text gives 3,260 kg at departure, while the handwritten chart shows 3,250 kg. [Merged chart readings](https://github.com/hound83/WW2-Aircraft-Physics-Simulator/blob/11395120a7c5ae3684c85a3b27d0b111df9a69e3/references/analysis/g6-16476-climb-charts.json) · [As-tested replay](https://github.com/hound83/WW2-Aircraft-Physics-Simulator/blob/11395120a7c5ae3684c85a3b27d0b111df9a69e3/validation/diagnostics/g6_archival_climb.cjs)

The subsequent pressure investigation isolated most of the excess time to the last kilometre. Using the factory manifold-pressure trace brought the 8–10 km time closer to the factory measurement while the model still climbed too quickly lower down. The combined benchmark tests the installed power and cooling assumptions behind those results. [Upper and lower interval analysis](https://github.com/hound83/WW2-Aircraft-Physics-Simulator/blob/11395120a7c5ae3684c85a3b27d0b111df9a69e3/docs/research/BF109G6_HIGH_ALTITUDE_CLIMB_2026-11-22.md)

#### B. P-51D level acceleration — test the shape of the replay

The printed Figure 8 in Ellis and Wheal’s 1990 report plots indicated speed at **60 seconds** against time to maximum level speed. For restored P-51D 45-11586 the merged reading is approximately **195–200 KIAS at 60 s**, with a printed maximum of **255 KIAS**. The wartime-profile proxy gives **217.38 proxy KIAS at 60 s**, about **17–22 knots high**. A secondary transcription supplies the separate 110–242 KIAS interval in 133 s. The simulation crosses that particular 242-KIAS target in 93.3 s. The previous draft assigned the secondary endpoint to the printed maximum. #### C. HF/LF Spitfire IX — a controlled test of relative accuracy

At 30,000 ft the paired Boscombe Down level-speed trial has the HF **18 mph TAS** faster than the LF. The model gives **29.7 mph**, overstating the difference by **11.7 mph**. The measured tables show three changes in the ordering, between 12,000–14,000 ft, 18,000–20,000 ft and 24,000–26,000 ft. Check where the model reproduces each band using BS.551 and BS.543 by supercharger gear. Then check the matching propeller and reduction-gear installation on a second HF airframe, EN.524, before attributing the difference to propeller efficiency. That aircraft had a reported carburettor change and its climb schedule partly draws on BS.551. Track those dependencies by metric. Report the signed HF–LF errors across altitude alongside the individual residuals. [Compared altitude bands](https://github.com/hound83/WW2-Aircraft-Physics-Simulator/blob/11395120a7c5ae3684c85a3b27d0b111df9a69e3/docs/research/WW2_COMBINED_FLIGHT_DATA_2026-11-22.md) · [Paired A&AEE trial](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitLF-HF-mkIX.html) · [Modeled differences](../V413_AIRCRAFT_INTAKE.md) · [EN.524 trial, §§4–5](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitHFmkIX_EN524.html)

#### D. Loaded-turn energy and stall — the longer research track

Start with Pekka Kokko’s MT-215 maneuver sheet. The two circulating accounts trace back to the same test and disagree on the date. Establish the altitude and settings of the straight acceleration intervals before pairing them with the turn. Seek a second airframe family with a recorded straight run and loaded run under overlapping conditions. The restored US campaign provides a qualified check. NACA W-6 on the F2A-3 provides an independent test of the calculation method. Preserve the actual aircraft, power, mass, speed basis, altitude history and cooling for each. Fit an added-drag term only when the straight-flight balance is constrained and a separate run or interval can test it. [MT-215 replay and source trail](../V414_POWERED_TURN_REPLAY.md) · [Finnish Air Force Museum research collections](https://airforcemuseum.fi/photo-and-research-collections/) · [NACA W-6](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930092640)

For clean stall, seek a low-speed position-error curve and run weight for P-51B 43-24777 or LF IX BS.543. Keep the indicated speed in the source units until the correction covers the observed speed. Use a sister-airframe weight transfer only within compatible wing geometry, configuration and lift evidence. Give clipped wings their own area and lift assumptions. Keep the current finite-turn estimates available while the historical comparison is unresolved. [Stall and lift evidence](../V412_CONTINUOUS_STALL.md) · [NACA TN 1044](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930084610)

#### E. Use paired engine and cooling tests to investigate persistent climb errors

The G-2 elapsed-climb replay averages **26.2 s slower** over five scored points. Check the source altitude convention, flight weight and climb-speed schedule before changing power. For Fw 190 A-4 WNr 783, compare open and closed cooling at the same intake and rating around the blower change. For P-38G 42-12687, use the part-power level runs to check calculated power and drag together. These cases can reveal when an apparent propeller-efficiency error can also be explained by cooling or by the assumed shaft power. Keep Greg’s drag estimates as a comparison and report which explanation survives a reserved flight condition.
