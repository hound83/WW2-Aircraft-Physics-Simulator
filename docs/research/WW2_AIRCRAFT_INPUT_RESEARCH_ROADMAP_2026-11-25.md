# Aircraft data research roadmap

25 November 2026 · Baseline 4.17.0-rc.1 · 14 operating aircraft

**Finish the current aircraft’s low-speed, acceleration and climb evidence first.** A new top-speed figure has less value than the test weight and airspeed correction that make an existing stall usable, or a timed run that reveals where an aircraft gains speed too quickly. The first new profile to prepare is **Spitfire I N.3171** to compare with the existing Bf 109 E-3. The first new aircraft family to prepare is the **F6F-3 Hellcat**. ## Phase 2 — Explain the remaining errors on the 14

Group the propulsion search by the missing quantity. The **G-6/Trop** and **HF IX** are two high-value checks for installed power at altitude. Look for shaft power at altitude, propeller efficiency at climb speed, or radiator drag at a recorded opening. A paired run with a changed cooling setting or a separate power measurement can distinguish explanations that fit the same speed. Then use measured loaded turns or a second lift coefficient to constrain drag in a turn. A straight acceleration interval alone cannot establish sustained turn performance. **When to add one.** Start with one identified configuration and one supported comparison. For speed/climb, recover geometry and loading, installation and rating, measured speeds and climb times over a stated altitude range, and reserve another condition or flight to check the result. A new profile can enter for that range while stall or turn remains open. A stall comparison additionally needs a known-weight, corrected measurement or an explicitly qualified same-wing transfer. Reserve fleet-wide expansion until the first new profile has passed its own check.

## How to collect the data once

For each number, capture aircraft mark, serial/block, wing and stores, event weight or bounded estimate, altitude, engine/propeller, rating, RPM, blower and cooling as applicable. Preserve the observed value, units and airspeed basis (indicated, calibrated, equivalent or true), any correction and its range, page/figure, and reading uncertainty. Retain the source’s own test conditions when a field is unknown. A handbook weight schedule or a level-speed-derived drag estimate is useful under its stated assumptions. Label each as measured, transferred or calculated, and record which observations set a parameter and which check it. Share weight-scaled stall data only when wing geometry, configuration and lift behavior support the transfer. Give a clipped wing its own evidence.

Search both the original reports and traceable secondary sources. For the French E-3, the original CEMA table puts the second 570 km/h point at 5,300 m, while Drix’s transcription gives 5,500 m. Keep both readings linked to one trial. Use Drix and Rocher for French leads, the Kokko transcriptions for the Finnish test sheet, and the Ellis–Wheal restored-aircraft article for its own test. Archive copies, translations and redrawn figures inherit their parent trial. Record an inaccessible page or unresolved conflict once so the next search starts at the missing measurement.
