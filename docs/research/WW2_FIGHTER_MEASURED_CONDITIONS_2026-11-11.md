## What the records add

- **P-47D-30 43-3441.** Eight steady-turn normal-force/elevator readings, four additional stall-approach windows and four explicitly calibrated NACA boom handling-test speeds. The longitudinal charts label their stall traces as service IAS. The separate calibrated boom readings start at 146 mph in the selected tests. They supply no near-stall service-ASI conversion. The original and reprint identify the tested propeller as four-blade Curtiss SPA-5. WR L-594 supplies 44 normal and military climb efficiency/coefficient readings on the three-blade experimental Hamilton Standard 6507A-2 with the same engine model. That comparison stays in augmented coverage. [TN 2899](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930083857) · [TN 2675](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930083825) · [WR L-594](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930093653)
- **P-47B 41-5902.** At 5,000 ft and 1,605 bhp/2,700 rpm, fully opening both oil cooler and intercooler flaps reduced speed from 323 to 309 mph TAS relative to neutral. Added the windshield comparison, a 665 bhp level-flight power point, and a normal-power climb where 20 mph more IAS improved cooling at a cost of 130 ft/min. [Wright Field PHQ-M-19-1417-A, pp. 2–3](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/p-47/p-47_files/P-47B_41-5902_PHQ-M-19-1417-A.pdf)
- **P-38G-1 42-12687.** Thirteen part-power level-flight points at 5,000 and 24,400 ft. Each carries TAS, engine RPM, chart BHP per engine and the estimated gross weight. The report flags the absence of torque meters, so the data constrain the combined propulsion and airframe balance. It judged induction intercooling adequate under the stated hot-day test conditions and the liquid coolant system inadequate. [Wright Field FS-M-19-1538-A, pp. 2–4](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/p-38/p-38_files/P-38G-1_42-12687_FS-M-19-1538-A.pdf)
- **Fw 190 A-4 WNr 783.** Factory tests quantify the cooling pressure and speed trade at 8,000 m and 2,400 rpm, plus the cooling-pressure adequacy thresholds for internal and external air intakes. Readings carry graph precision. The GM 1 extension is the factory's conditional calculation. [Focke-Wulf 783 Nr. 4](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/fw190/a4/a4_files/Fw_190_783_Nr4.pdf)
- **Spitfire LF IX and Fw 190 D-9.** JL165 provides 17 paired radiator-open/shut climb points at 7,400 lb. At 11,400 ft, 3,000 rpm and +25 lb/sq.in. the report gives 4,335 versus 4,750 ft/min. WNr 210006 gives a radiator-position speed sweep and an empty-tank penalty curve. Its best radiator position was around 2–3 tenths open for the tested 2,000 m, 2,700 rpm condition. The effects are specific to the test installations. [A&AEE JL165](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitIX_25boost_JL165.html) · [Rechlin report 9003](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/fw190/d9/d9_files/Fw_190_D-9_006.pdf)
- **P-40F 41-13635.** Rechecked seven matched brace-on/off speed pairs and corrected the 1,100 chart-BHP value to 369.5 versus 374 mph TAS. The paired speed losses span 4.5–8.0 mph. The added power-equilibrium calculation varies propulsive power and the induced-drag share explicitly, so the range represents assumptions applied to measured speeds. [Wright Field FS-M-19-1578-A, p. 1](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/p-40/p-40_files/P-40F_41-13635_FS-M-19-1578-A.pdf)
- **Bf 109 G-2.** Messerschmitt's original G-1 WNr 14001 cooling report gives 43 km/h speed loss at full radiator flap opening and +42 °C ambient. The English mirror says 48 km/h. The original German value is used, as a comparison for early DB 605 A cooling. Existing documented G-2 ratings still govern the G-2 benchmark. [Messerschmitt 109 18 L 42](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/me109/g/g_files/VB-109-18-L-42.pdf)
- **Ki-84 T-2 302.** The original prints “20 mm Hg” both before and after a stated power reduction. The earlier inch-of-mercury assumption was removed from the registered rows. The 79 mph landing stall and the separate 81/74 mph control limits retain their IAS basis. [AAF interim report](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/japan/T-2_Report_on_Frank_I_Ki-84.pdf)

## Remaining source gaps

- No maximum sustained-turn measurement was added. The P-47D-30 turns supply steady maneuver control data without a maximum-performance claim. The loaded stall approaches are transient.
- The P-47B's same-serial position-error table starts at 170 mph IAS. The P-38G-1 installation table starts at 150 mph IAS. No matching new near-stall correction was found in NACA TR 919 or TN 1892.
- The Spitfire BF274 airspeed correction chart and its applicability close to stall remain open. RAE ARC 7424 is still a report locator. The highest-value UK retrieval target is A&AEE 692/m Part III Figure 3 and its original limits.
- The NII VVS historical narrative identifies Fw 190 A-4 WNr 2310 and WNr 2362 as Soviet test specimens. An original quantitative turn record for either specimen remains to be recovered. The Bf 109 F-4 EB-100 identification and original U.S. flight report also remain open.
- Next score gains depend on matching low-speed calibration sheets, whole-airframe drag measurements with power context, and loaded turns with documented altitude and speed retention. The scorecard lists the specific retrieval target per aircraft.
