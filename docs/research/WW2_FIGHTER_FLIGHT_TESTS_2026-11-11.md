# Fighter operating data and international flight-test reports

11 November 2026. This research checkpoint covers thirteen fighter variants. It records 70 data rows in 13 series and tracks 23 source entries, including companion reports, prior sources rechecked, and unclosed leads. The ten visually digitized windows come from two existing NACA test programs. Archived links and scan hashes identify the sources.

## What the reports establish

- **United States.** Five F6F-3 first-departure windows and five P-47D-30 stall or approach windows were read from the original figures. The Hellcat clean, idle glide trace reads approximately 97 mph at initial roll-off. The Part III axis says indicated airspeed. Part I defines its own position-corrected service indicated speed, and the processing link and run weights for Part III still need confirmation. The enlarged Part III approach row reads 2,550 rpm and 23 inHg, correcting the previous 2,350 rpm transcription. P-47D-30 Figure 52 has one trace labeled service indicated airspeed. Its NACA emblem was previously misread as a second trace. The figure shows approximately 91 mph near the minimum approach speed at 12,860 lb and 5,000 ft. A full break is unclear. TN 2675 describes the calibrated research boom, with the exact processing used for the L8A06 trace still to verify. [Hellcat Part III](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930092602) · [Thunderbolt RM L8A06](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090022749) · [TN 2675](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930083825)
- **United States, P-40F/L.** T.O. 01-25CH-1 provides four approximate operational stall IAS values from 80 to 94 mph, loading examples, and V-1650-1 ratings. The chart gives 48.2 inHg at 3,000 rpm for five minutes at military power and 44.2 inHg at 2,650 rpm continuously. The 61 inHg war-emergency entry requires a boost-control modification. The manual's 92 mph clean IAS lacks a serial, actual test weight and correction curve. NACA WR L-547 uses a research pitot-static installation, which must remain separate from the service IAS. [Flight instructions](https://aviationarchives.blogspot.com/2022/01/curtiss-p-40fl-warhawk-flight.html) · [NACA WR L-547](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930092648)
- **Britain.** The original BS543 combat-climb plot confirms a Merlin 66, about 7,485 lb at take-off and open radiator flaps. The text transcription documents the use of the matching BF274 pressure-head correction. The measured 130–320 mph range and the lower extrapolated entries were already registered, and neither covers the stall region. The MA648 trial is a separate installation with an S.U. injection pump and altered intake. The RAE bibliography also points to Aero 1906 / ARC 7424 for high-Mach fighter drag and trim. The original report and the BF274 correction plot remain open. [BS543 trial](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitLF-HF-mkIX.html) · [BF274](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitFmkIX_BF274.html) · [MA648](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk9/spitLFMkIX_MA648.html) · [ARC R&M 2222](https://reports.aerade.cranfield.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/1826.2/3233/arc-rm-2222.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **Germany and Finland.** The June 1942 G-2 manual directs take-off at 1.30 ata and 2,600 rpm and explicitly blocks the listed 1.42 ata and 2,800 rpm setting. The MT-215 flight transcription continues to show 2,540 actual rpm and approximately 1.30–1.32 ata below critical height. Its original numerical tables remain outstanding. Focke-Wulf D-9 Nr. 4 gives the replacement engine's 1.40 ata combat critical heights of 2,550 m in climb and 3,350 m in level flight. The airframe had a sealed engine gap, polished finish and guns without ammunition. Nr. 1 documents a different specimen's Eltron/Bruhn installation comparison at higher speeds. Two original A-4 reports document 2,400 rpm Eastern Front fairing tests on WNr 665 and a WNr 670 Jabo tank trial limited by aileron buffet at 380 km/h indicated. [G-2 factory instructions](https://stephentaylorhistorian.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/bf-109g-2.pdf) · [MT-215](https://kurfurst.org/Performance_tests/109G_MT215/109G2_MT215_en.html) · [D-9 factory reports](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/fw190/d9/fw190d9.html) · [A-4 WNr 665](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/fw190/a4/a4_files/Fw_190_665_Nr1.pdf) · [A-4 WNr 670](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/fw190/a4/a4_files/Fw_190_670_Nr1.pdf)
- **USSR.** The NII VVS WNr 14513 report is already registered as a gunpod G-2 trial. The December 1943 digest names a different Fw 190 A-4, WNr 2310. Its original run sheets, mass and test power are still needed before numerical comparison to WNr 783. [G-2 original scan](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/me109/g/g_files/Me-109_G2_14513_Russian.pdf) · [A-4 digest reproduction](https://military.wikireading.ru/arjaMGGPIg)
- **Japan.** The Smithsonian holds the July 1945 PACMIRS translation of captured Japanese Ki-84 instructions. Printed pages 28–29 give six stall figures, twelve standard maximum-speed rows and nine climb points. The 624 km/h entry at 6,550 m gives 2,900 rpm and leaves boost blank. The stall table gives 165 km/h at 2,000 m clean with the airframe fully equipped, with no numerical mass or explicit speed basis. No serial or measured-flight provenance is identified in these tables. The translator explicitly links some tables to ADVATIS Translation 92. The Japanese originals and any serial 302 performance measurement remain open. [Smithsonian catalogue](https://www.si.edu/object/archives/components/sova-nasm-xxxx-0428-ref4016) · [PACMIRS scan](https://www.si.edu/media/NASM/NASM-Manual-000033269.pdf) · [NIDS catalogue](https://www.nids.mod.go.jp/military_archives/pdf/catalog/a2.pdf)
