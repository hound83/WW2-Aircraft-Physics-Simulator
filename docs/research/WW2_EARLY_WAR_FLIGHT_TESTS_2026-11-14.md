# Early-war flight-test evidence for the next aircraft batch

Research handoff, 14 November 2026. The two batch aircraft are **Bf 109 E-3 WNr 1304 / AE479** and **Spitfire Va X.4922**. **Spitfire I N.3171** is the follow-on. The [numeric extract](../../references/analysis/ww2_early_war_flight_tests_2026-11-14.json) contains 41 series, 242 rows, and 24 source records. The source list includes companion reports and archive leads, and several publications share the same trial lineage. The extract is a research input for selecting benchmarks.

## Spitfire Va X.4922

The A&AEE report and its June corrigendum establish a trial weight of 6,450 lb, CG 6.2 in aft of datum, Merlin XLV, an eight-Browning wing and a de Havilland propeller with 34° fine and 54° coarse stops. The extract includes every row of the level and climb tables, including the zero-altitude clock origin and the separately listed climb full-throttle row. Level speed was 337 mph TAS at 10,000 ft and peaked at 375 mph TAS at 20,800 ft, at +9 lb/in² and 3,000 rpm. Climb at +9 and 2,850 rpm peaked at 3,140 ft/min at 14,400 ft and took 7.1 min to 20,000 ft. The recorded rows bracket the high-altitude change to 3,000 rpm between 23,000 and 26,000 ft. The summary and detailed table give different service ceilings, 37,700 and 37,850 ft. Both are preserved. [A&AEE X.4922 report](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk5/spitmkVa_X4922.html)

The closest stall donor recovered is another full-span Va, NACA W.3119, at approximately 6,184 lb and 31.4% MAC, with a Rotol propeller. L-353 reports clean-glide maximum lift coefficients of 1.18 with covered gun ports and 1.15 with open ports, and labels the corresponding 90 and 91 mph values as indicated. L-534 discusses cockpit ASI error near minimum speed, so the indicated values retain their original label. The extract includes ten stall cases, covering glide, powered conditions, landing and takeoff. The recorded accelerated-turn maximum lift coefficient of 1.22 supplies a lift reference. With the donor's 242 ft² wing area and each measured glide lift coefficient, the projection at X.4922's test weight is about 94.0 or 95.2 mph EAS for the two gun-port cases. These are calculated scenarios conditional on the wing, CG, configuration and maximum lift carrying over. Keep the powered stall cases tied to the W.3119 installation. [NACA L-353](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930092581/downloads/19930092581.pdf) · [NACA L-534](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930092582/downloads/19930092582.pdf) · [W.3119 serial history](https://caspir.warplane.com/aircraft/serial-search/aircraft-no/200001558/group/W3109_W3970)

The A&AEE Vb W.3134 provides a same-engine cross-check with its own cannon-wing installation, test weight of 6,525 lb and separate snowguard trials. The clean aircraft reached 371 mph TAS at 20,100 ft and 3,250 ft/min at 15,200 ft. The snowguard configuration peaked at 365 mph at 18,800 ft. The detailed level curves are valuable for checking intake losses. The two reports also quote different K.9788 speed figures and the X.4922 report prints two K.9788 weights. The extract keeps those report-specific values with their source. L-534 roll-test figures 25–28 are a lead for later digitizing. [A&AEE W.3134 report](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk5/spitmkVb_W3134.html) · [A&AEE X.4922 comparison](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk5/spitmkVa_X4922.html) · [NACA L-534](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930092582/downloads/19930092582.pdf)

## Bf 109 E-3 WNr 1304 / AE479

The archive identifies CEMA report No. 371, December 1939, as a direct test of WNr 1304. The readable Drix transcription gives eight cumulative climb times from 1,000 to 8,000 m and eleven level-speed values across radiator states. Radiator open reaches 530 km/h at 4,500 m. The closed readings are 570 km/h at 5,000 and 5,500 m. Rocher reproduces the French account of 530 km/h at 4,500 m and quotes 6 min 20 s to 5,000 m in the CEAM comparison with the D.520. The agreement helps trace the figures to period work. The publications may share measurements. The CEMA chart and British reproduction were located as scans. The point-by-point numbers here come from readable transcriptions and period excerpts. Williams compiles a 342–354 mph peak range from the French lineage and notes estimated position error. [CEMA report index](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/me109e.html) · [Original chart](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/Me-109E3-French.jpg) · [British chart reproduction](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/Me-109E3-French-2.jpg) · [Drix transcription](https://aviadrix.blogspot.com/2012/09/messerschmitt-109-emile-maitre-de-la.html) · [Rocher, pp. 22–24](https://www.docdroid.net/file/download/dGVG1So/le-bf-109-face-aux-chasseurs-francais-le-fana-de-laviation-2018-12-pdf.pdf) · [Williams comparison](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/mk1-109e.html)

Use the cumulative clocks for a provisional climb replay. The stated 7,000–8,000 m band average is 4.7 m/s, while the clocks at those endpoints imply 6.49 m/s. Both readings are in the extract. The run mass, exact power and rpm, clock start and climb speed remain open. Keep the two radiator curves separate pending verification of the airspeed calibration, power and test duration. Rocher also reports a separate CEAM estimate of a 20–30 km/h gain from closing the radiator at 600 m. Treat it as context for the cooling drag sensitivity. [Drix transcription](https://aviadrix.blogspot.com/2012/09/messerschmitt-109-emile-maitre-de-la.html) · [Rocher, pp. 22–24](https://www.docdroid.net/file/download/dGVG1So/le-bf-109-face-aux-chasseurs-francais-le-fana-de-laviation-2018-12-pdf.pdf)

For the low-altitude speed hole, use factory E-3 WNr 1792 first after verifying the wing-armament configuration in its original test. Williams reads 290 mph at sea level at 1.30 ata. The U.S. E-3 memorandum supplies 290 mph at sea level and 339 mph at 17,500 ft at 1.30 ata in the secondary compilation. AE479 arrived at Wright Field in 1942 after a 1941 tail repair using parts from WNr 1480. The U.S. memorandum may therefore cover a later configuration of AE479. Keep it as a provisional cross-check until the report serial and configuration are verified. Other German readings and the Swiss DB 601 Aa aircraft provide context. The DB 601 A handbook provides engine limits and power points tied to DB 601 A ratings. Its 5-minute table gives 990 PS near the ground and 1,020 PS at 4,500 m at 1.30 ata and 2,400 rpm. Whether that rating was used in each French run is open. [Factory WNr 1792 chart](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/me109e3-1792.jpg) · [Wright Field E-3 chart](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/Me-109E3-US.jpg) · [USAF Museum AE479 photo](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Upcoming/Photos/igphoto/2000571943/) · [AE479 serial history](https://www.joebaugher.com/usaf_serials/captured_axis_aircraft.html) · [Williams comparison](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/mk1-109e.html) · [DB 601 handbook, p. 21](https://www.pumaszallas.hu/Private/VO101_Tom/docs/DB601_AuB_MM_1940_08.pdf)

The British glide-stall reproduction preserves 75 mph with flaps up and 61 mph with flaps down. The exact weight, gear configuration and airspeed reduction for those two numbers remain open. Keep the existing 890-ft turn-radius record in the [priority comparisons](../../references/research/ww2_priority_comparative_2026-11-13.json) and use the RAE K.9791 comparison for relative climb and handling context. The K.9791 observations apply to its specific comparison with WNr 1304. E-series slatted-wing data stay within the E donor track. ## Follow-on Spitfire I N.3171

The March 1940 A&AEE trial identifies a 6,050-lb Merlin III aircraft with a three-blade 10 ft 9 in Rotol, projecting armoured windscreen, tank armour and domed hood. The extract retains eleven level-speed rows, sixteen climb rows, propeller comparisons, load schedule and field performance. The test reached 354 mph TAS at 18,900 ft at the nominal +6¼ lb/in², 3,000-rpm rating. The recommended-speed climb took 3.5 min to 10,000 ft and 7.7 min to 20,000 ft and peaked at 2,905 ft/min at 11,000 ft. The maximum-climb and recommended schedules were distinct, with initial indicated speeds of 140 and 162 mph respectively. The two 15,000-ft summary/detail discrepancies are retained. The summary's flap-up and flap-down stall observations are 78 and 68 mph at the test weight, with speed basis and power unstated alongside them. [A&AEE N.3171 report](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/spitmk1_N.3171.html)

The comparative compilation gives 283 mph at sea level for N.3171 at the same nominal level rating. This is a provisional low-altitude point pending a chart read. The July R.6774 de Havilland case gives a propeller cross-check, and its quoted N.3171 values share the earlier Rotol lineage. An RAE +12 lb/in² case at 314 mph sea level and 359 mph at 11,500 ft is recorded separately with airframe attribution unresolved. Use the March Rotol configuration as the first implementation case. [A&AEE comparative archive](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/spitmk1.html) · [Williams comparison](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/mk1-109e.html)

## Benchmarks to stage first

1. Stage X.4922's measured level-speed and climb curves. Use W.3119's clean-glide coefficients for stall sensitivity only after verifying configuration and CG compatibility.
2. Stage WNr 1304's French elapsed climb provisionally. Compare the radiator-open level series to the factory E-3 speed checks and the handbook rating. Treat radiator-closed speed as a separate test case until the original reductions and conditions are recovered.
3. Stage N.3171's measured Rotol curves as the follow-on. Retain the stall-speed observations in their reported basis until the report or calibration evidence resolves the channel. Use the dated engine ratings and companion sources for any separate emergency-boost case.
