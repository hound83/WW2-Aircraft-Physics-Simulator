# Early-war flight-test evidence for the next aircraft batch

Research handoff, 14 November 2026, extended 16 November 2026. The two batch aircraft are **Bf 109 E-3 WNr 1304 / AE479** and **Spitfire Va X.4922**. **Spitfire I N.3171** is the follow-on. The [numeric extract](../../references/analysis/ww2_early_war_flight_tests_2026-11-14.json) contains 78 series, 522 rows, and 40 source records. The source list includes companion reports and archive leads, and several publications share the same trial lineage. The extract is a research input for selecting benchmarks.

| Airframe | Use first | Data to settle before stronger claims |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spitfire Va X.4922 | Exact A&AEE level, climb and takeoff trials. NACA W.3119 clean glide lift as a conditional stall donor. | Exact stall, sub-10,000-ft speed, and full Merlin 45 power and propeller data for this installation. |
| Bf 109 E-3 WNr 1304 | Original CEMA radiator-state speed tables and RAE reproduction of the French climb. Factory E-3 WNr 1792 is the first low-altitude cross-check. | Airspeed calibration, per-run weight and airspeed schedule, and calibrated stall basis. |
| Spitfire I N.3171 | Exact March 1940 A&AEE Rotol +6¼ lb/in² case. Retain reported flap-up and flap-down stall observations. | Original low-altitude speed chart and stall instrument/power basis. The +12 lb/in² case has its own evidence. |

## Spitfire Va X.4922

The A&AEE report and its June corrigendum establish a trial weight of 6,450 lb, CG 6.2 in aft of datum, Merlin XLV, an eight-Browning wing and a de Havilland propeller with 34° fine and 54° coarse stops. The extract includes every row of the level and climb tables, including the zero-altitude clock origin and the separately listed climb full-throttle row. Level speed was 337 mph TAS at 10,000 ft and peaked at 375 mph TAS at 20,800 ft, at +9 lb/in² and 3,000 rpm. Climb at +9 and 2,850 rpm peaked at 3,140 ft/min at 14,400 ft and took 7.1 min to 20,000 ft. The recorded rows bracket the high-altitude change to 3,000 rpm between 23,000 and 26,000 ft. The summary and detailed table give different service ceilings, 37,700 and 37,850 ft. Both are preserved. [A&AEE X.4922 report](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk5/spitmkVa_X4922.html)

The closest stall donor recovered is another full-span Va, NACA W.3119, at approximately 6,184 lb and 31.4% MAC, with a Rotol propeller. L-353 reports clean-glide maximum lift coefficients of 1.18 with covered gun ports and 1.15 with open ports, and labels the corresponding 90 and 91 mph values as indicated. L-334 discusses cockpit ASI error near minimum speed, so the indicated values retain their original label. The extract includes ten stall cases, covering glide, powered conditions, landing and takeoff. The recorded accelerated-turn maximum lift coefficient of 1.22 supplies a lift reference. With the donor's 242 ft² wing area and each measured glide lift coefficient, the projection at X.4922's test weight is about 94.0 or 95.2 mph EAS for the two gun-port cases. These are calculated scenarios conditional on the wing, CG, configuration and maximum lift carrying over. Keep the powered stall cases tied to the W.3119 installation. [NACA L-353](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930092581/downloads/19930092581.pdf) · [NACA L-334](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930092582/downloads/19930092582.pdf) · [W.3119 serial history](https://caspir.warplane.com/aircraft/serial-search/aircraft-no/200001558/group/W3109_W3970)

The A&AEE Vb W.3134 provides a same-engine cross-check with its own cannon-wing installation, test weight of 6,525 lb and separate snowguard trials. The clean aircraft reached 371 mph TAS at 20,100 ft and 3,250 ft/min at 15,200 ft. The snowguard configuration peaked at 365 mph at 18,800 ft. The detailed level curves are valuable for checking intake losses. The two reports also quote different K.9788 speed figures and the X.4922 report prints two K.9788 weights. The extract keeps those report-specific values with their source. L-334 Fig. 27 has now been digitized, with the report’s roll references in the extract. [A&AEE W.3134 report](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk5/spitmkVb_W3134.html) · [A&AEE X.4922 comparison](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk5/spitmkVa_X4922.html) · [NACA L-334](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930092582/downloads/19930092582.pdf)

## Bf 109 E-3 WNr 1304 / AE479

CEMA report No. 371, December 1939, supplies **12 level-speed points** in the original p. 4 table. The seven open-radiator readings cover 2,500–5,500 m and peak at **530 km/h at 4,500 m**. The five closed-radiator readings cover 5,000–7,000 m and give **570 km/h at 5,000 and 5,300 m**. The original scan places the second 570 km/h point **200 m below** the Drix transcription and adds **512 km/h with radiators open at 5,500 m**. All 12 are at 2,400 rpm. The source assumes a unity position factor because the aircraft had yet to fly a calibration course. It estimates 2–3% speed uncertainty, including ±15 km/h at the peak. The open and closed cases at 5,000 m read 520 and 570 km/h with manifold pressures of 870 and 880 mm respectively. Use the 50 km/h observed difference as a sensitivity check and preserve the pressure difference. The labeled WNr 1304 chart peaks around 550 km/h and shows a drawn trend below the lowest p. 4 tabular run. Its radiator configuration and relation to the tables are open. [CEMA p. 4](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/Me-109E3-French-3.jpg) · [CEMA chart](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/Me-109E3-French.jpg) · [Drix transcription](https://aviadrix.blogspot.com/2012/09/messerschmitt-109-emile-maitre-de-la.html)

The RAE’s period reproduction identifies the French trial weight as **5,600 lb** and supplies **eight climb rows** with elapsed time, point climb rate, rpm and boost. The radiators were open to 13,000 ft and gradually closed through 26,000 ft. Use **6:20, 8:01 and 10:02** at the nominal 5,000, 6,000 and 7,000 m levels, with the original RAE altitude axis used in the extract. Drix agrees at 5,000 m and gives 8:16 and 11:01 at 6,000 and 7,000 m. Drix’s final time is 13:35, which matches the RAE last row near 8,000 m. The RAE table reports point climb rates, while the secondary table quotes band means. The disagreement in the clocks remains separate from that distinction. The exact clock start and climb airspeed remain open. Rocher corroborates 6:20 to 5,000 m in the published CEAM comparison with the D.520 and cites a 20–30 km/h cooling-shutter gain estimate at 600 m. The RAE and secondary sources may share the same flights. [RAE §2.2 reproduction](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/Me-109E3-French-2.jpg) · [Rocher, pp. 22–26](https://www.docdroid.net/file/download/dGVG1So/le-bf-109-face-aux-chasseurs-francais-le-fana-de-laviation-2018-12-pdf.pdf)

For the low-altitude speed hole, use factory E-3 WNr 1792 first. Four timed runs reduce to **466–467 km/h at sea level** at a reported manifold pressure of 1.30 kg/cm², with the DB 601 A, three-blade VDM, extended tailwheel and radiators 55 mm open. Two runs used MG FF mock-ups and two used capped ports. The near-identical speeds apply to those mock-up and cap configurations. The U.S. E-3 memorandum supplies 290 mph at sea level and 339 mph at 17,500 ft at 1.30 ata in the secondary compilation. AE479 arrived at Wright Field in 1942 after a 1941 tail repair using parts from WNr 1480. The U.S. memorandum may therefore cover a later configuration of AE479. Keep it as a provisional cross-check until the report serial and configuration are verified. Other German readings and the Swiss DB 601 Aa aircraft provide context. The DB 601 A handbook provides engine limits and power points tied to DB 601 A ratings. Its 5-minute table gives 990 PS near the ground and 1,020 PS at 4,500 m at 1.30 ata and 2,400 rpm. Whether that rating was used in each French run is open. [Factory WNr 1792 chart](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/me109e3-1792.jpg) · [Wright Field E-3 chart](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/Me-109E3-US.jpg) · [USAF Museum AE479 photo](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Upcoming/Photos/igphoto/2000571943/) · [AE479 serial history](https://www.joebaugher.com/usaf_serials/captured_axis_aircraft.html) · [Williams comparison](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/mk1-109e.html) · [DB 601 handbook, p. 21](https://www.pumaszallas.hu/Private/VO101_Tom/docs/DB601_AuB_MM_1940_08.pdf)

Two British reports give separate stall runs. A&AEE/755 part 1 §2.5 reports **130 km/h (81 mph) A.S.I.R. with gear and flaps up**, and **100 km/h (62 mph) with both down**. The RAE glide account gives 75 and 61 mph with flaps up and down respectively. Neither source establishes the stall-run weight and corrected airspeed for its pair. The A&AEE report leaves power unstated beside the stall figures. Its 81 mph figure belongs to the original raw reading and cannot directly set clean EAS or maximum lift. The same report puts the onset of heavy controls around 250 mph and describes difficult dive recovery around 400 mph, with the speed basis unspecified beside those handling observations. Keep the existing 890-ft turn-radius record in the [priority comparisons](../../references/research/ww2_priority_comparative_2026-11-13.json) and use the RAE K.9791 comparison for relative climb and handling context. The K.9791 observations apply to its specific comparison with WNr 1304. E-series slatted-wing data stay within the E donor track. [Morgan and Smelt bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0368393100119996) · [British report reproduction](https://www.pilotfriend.com/flight_reports/reports/33.htm) · [A&AEE handling scan](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/me109/me109e-aaee.pdf) · [RAE comparison excerpt](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/spitmk1.html)

| **E-series handbook and DB 601 manuals** | The E handbook gives a best-climb indicated schedule from **250 km/h at sea level to 200 km/h at 7,000 m**. It supplies a practical sensitivity case for the unrecorded French climb speed. It also gives **1.30 ata at 2,400 rpm for 5 minutes** and **1.20 ata at 2,300 rpm for 30 minutes** below about 5 km. ## Follow-on Spitfire I N.3171

The March 1940 A&AEE trial identifies a 6,050-lb Merlin III aircraft with a three-blade 10 ft 9 in Rotol, projecting armoured windscreen, tank armour and domed hood. The extract retains eleven level-speed rows, sixteen climb rows, propeller comparisons, load schedule and field performance. The test reached 354 mph TAS at 18,900 ft at the nominal +6¼ lb/in², 3,000-rpm rating. The recommended-speed climb took 3.5 min to 10,000 ft and 7.7 min to 20,000 ft and peaked at 2,905 ft/min at 11,000 ft. The maximum-climb and recommended schedules were distinct, with initial indicated speeds of 140 and 162 mph respectively. The two 15,000-ft summary/detail discrepancies are retained. The summary's flap-up and flap-down stall observations are 78 and 68 mph at the test weight, with speed basis and power unstated alongside them. [A&AEE N.3171 report](https://wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/spitmk1_N.3171.html)

The comparative compilation gives 283 mph at sea level for N.3171 at the same nominal level rating. This is a provisional low-altitude point pending a chart read. The July R.6774 de Havilland case gives a propeller cross-check, and its quoted N.3171 values share the earlier Rotol lineage. An RAE +12 lb/in² case at 314 mph sea level and 359 mph at 11,500 ft is recorded separately with airframe attribution unresolved. Use the March Rotol configuration as the first implementation case. [A&AEE comparative archive](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/spitmk1.html) · [Williams comparison](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.com/org/spitcom/mk1/mk1-109e.html)

## Benchmarks to stage first

1. Stage X.4922's measured +9 level-speed and climb curves. Check low-altitude extrapolation against the RAE Mk V chart as a family comparison. Use W.3119's clean-glide coefficients and force-limited roll as provisional same-mark references with configuration checks.
2. Stage WNr 1304's 5,600 lb French climb and open-radiator speed with the original settings and airspeed uncertainty. Use the separate closed-radiator speed series for cooling sensitivity and WNr 1792 for a low-altitude check. Keep both British stall runs on their reported speed scales.
3. Stage N.3171's measured Rotol curves as the follow-on. Retain the stall-speed observations in their reported basis until the report or calibration evidence resolves the channel. Use the dated engine ratings and companion sources for any separate emergency-boost case.
