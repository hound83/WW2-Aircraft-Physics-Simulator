# Simulator roadmap

25 November 2026 · Starting point: [4.16 fourteen-aircraft benchmark](V416_PERFORMANCE_BENCHMARK.md)

**Milestones 1 and 2 delivered together in [4.17](V417_EVIDENCE_AND_PERFORMANCE.md). Next is clean stall and straight-line acceleration (milestone 3).** The original scope was to establish what we can trust about the 14 aircraft already in the simulator. First collect missing measurements. Then use them to check level speed and climb across altitude. Start collecting roll rate in the same sweep. Aircraft comparisons should tell the reader how large the observed difference is, at which speeds or altitudes it holds, and whether the model reproduces it.

### How roll enters a comparison

Show achieved roll rate in degrees per second against airspeed, with direction when reported. Give one measured point one marker. Draw a curve only across speeds covered by comparable observations. Distinguish full-deflection or demonstrated-maximum results from runs flown at a specified force or partial deflection. Put test force in the source note. A 30 lb measurement alone cannot establish what an aircraft could achieve at 80 lb. This roadmap requires no pilot-strength parameter.

### Source order

Use the original test, chart, handbook or manufacturer's engine record first, retaining the exact page and distinguishing flight measurements, estimates and handbook limits. If the original is missing, use traceable secondary transcriptions and follow their references, including Wikipedia's references where useful. Identify duplicate accounts of the same flight. IL-2 and DCS can supply a labeled simulator comparison or a search lead. Historical accuracy and coverage figures count historical observations only. Report a near-match where it helps and make the configuration difference visible.
